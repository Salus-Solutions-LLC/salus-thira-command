# Salus Crisis Communications Planning Standard

**Requirement:** SALUS-CRISIS-COMMS-PLAN-001. **Decision:** SAL-D152. **Owner direction:** 23 November 2026. Apply with `SALUS_PLANNING_STANDARD.md` P01–P12 and existing Salus authorities. This member-neutral module is a governed candidate until promotion; it is not an operational order or a populated Member plan.

## Purpose and complete communication cycle

Plan how the organization prepares, hears concerns and warnings, starts its team, verifies facts, reaches affected people, listens and corrects errors, sustains updates, supports recovery and learns. Address safety emergencies and relevant conduct, safeguarding, financial, cyber/privacy, public-health, service-loss and trust crises. The assessment determines actual applicability and priority; listing an event does not claim it occurred. The communications plan references approved protective actions and response/continuity plans without inventing tactics, emergency powers or local duties.

## C01 Start rules and authority

Set observable activation/escalation triggers and distinguish developing concern, urgent warning, sustained crisis updates and recovery. Identify who can activate each route, its primary/alternate approver, limits and review time, and the safe fallback when the lead is absent or conflicted. Establish distinct authority paths for immediate life-safety warning, required reports, private family notification and public statements. Public-message clearance must not obstruct urgent help, lawful personal reporting or mandatory reporting. Confirm actual legal duties before use; communications authority is not incident command authority.

## C01A Incident tempo and parallel procedures

Choose procedures by time to harm or a missed duty, rate of change, evidence uncertainty, audience needs and decision lead time, rather than the incident label alone. Use distinct routes for immediate danger, rapidly changing events, concerns allowing a structured fact check, and sustained response/recovery. For each route specify activation, lead/backup, first tasks, permitted pre-cleared messages, approval path and fallback, public/private/required-report audiences, update cadence, verification depth, staffing, escalation/de-escalation conditions and review proof. Set actual deadlines and justified response targets with competent owners; do not invent universal minutes/hours or use a slower classification to postpone a binding notice or urgent care.

Run parallel tracks when needed: issue a narrowly scoped authorized warning or required report while separately verifying causes, wider facts and public statements. Verification is proportionate to the specific message and consequence; urgency never permits knowingly false content, while uncertainty about cause must not delay an otherwise justified safety action. Reassess tempo at each significant new fact or missed update. Escalate on increased danger, exposed people, fast spread, approaching legal/contract deadlines, harmful misinformation or channel/lead failure. De-escalation needs a named authority, verified basis, residual duties, continued care and next review. Test a slow concern becoming urgent, an urgent event entering sustained response and a new urgent event during recovery. The approved scenario response plan and competent authority determine actual protective actions.

## C02 Roles and sustained cover

Assign the team lead, fact lead, message writer, approver, public spokesperson, affected-person care/family liaison, channel operator, partner liaison and recorder, with backups and contact routes. Roles may be combined only after workload, conflicts and capacity are checked. Cover nights/weekends, leader unavailability, shift transfer, fatigue and concurrent demands. Preserve one approved fact/message set with several authorized delivery roles, avoiding both inconsistent statements and a single-person bottleneck. Protect staff's lawful reporting and personal rights; a spokesperson rule is not a gag on protected reports.

## C03 Fact verification and uncertainty

Log the report, source, place and observed/reported time. Verify decision-changing facts with the competent source and preserve provenance. Mark checked facts, unverified reports, unknowns and working assumptions distinctly. Track contradictory evidence and who will resolve it. State what is known, what is not known, what is being done and the next update. Do not speculate about identity, cause, guilt, casualty count, motive, clinical condition or responsibility. Pre-approved holding messages may acknowledge a verified event and safe action without pretending all facts are known; define actual sender authority and conditions before use.

## C04 Audiences and affected-person handoffs

Map people in danger, people harmed and families, children/caregivers, visitors, staff/volunteers, leadership and other locations, responders/public health/other competent authorities, insurers/suppliers, neighbors, donors, news media and the public where relevant. For each, record its distinct needs, trusted messenger, privacy boundaries, message priority, language/access needs, delivery and feedback route, and owner. Distinguish receipt from comprehension and action. A handoff names the receiving role, accepted responsibility, retained duties and authorized release; a sent notice or arrival alone proves no completed transfer. Sensitive family contact and child reunification information require verified identity and authorized disclosure.

## C05 Message design and approval

Use third-grade plain language for concepts, instructions and templates. Include the trusted sender, specific affected place/group, required safe action and timing, checked facts, uncertainty, appropriate care/help route, and next update time/channel. Put urgent action first when needed. Match the message to its audience and medium without changing the facts. Preserve exact dates, time zones, message version and approval in the record. Verify placeholders and links before release; never transmit an unfilled template as a live message. Record when the update is due and escalate missed commitments rather than silently going quiet. Avoid unsupported reassurance or promises.

## C06 Channels and failure modes

Map each channel's owner/access authority, intended group, consent/delivery rules, capacity, coverage, access needs, dependency, failure indication, backup and test proof. Consider voice/public address, visual/text alerts, radio, calls, SMS, email, website, social media, print/signs and trusted in-person routes as appropriate. Do not assume a device, app, internet access, public alert subscription or public authority's warning service covers all needs. Check hearing, vision, language, literacy and other access needs; test translations and relevant audio/text alternatives.

Test loss of power, network, account, supplier and primary lead; overload; stale contact lists; failed sends; and two channels sharing the same failure. Maintain approved offline role/contact aids and a safe alternative route. Protect accounts and delegated access, verify genuine messages through trusted routes, and keep credentials in the approved secret store. Plan for spoofed messages, malicious links, account compromise and impersonation without inferring that they occurred. No automated collection or monitoring is authorized by writing the plan.

## C07 Public and media communication

Train and support the authorized spokesperson with a current fact sheet, key actions, likely questions, known limits, next briefing and specialist referral routes. Log inquiry, source, promised response and follow-up. Coordinate with competent partners while respecting each one's mandate; do not imply authority to speak for emergency services or an investigator. State a factual reason when information cannot yet be shared, what can be said and when an update is expected. Use fair, respectful, accurate language and explain verified corrective action. Protect affected people and operations during briefings; do not invent findings or promises to protect reputation.

## C08 Listening correction and rumor triage

Define lawful and authorized ways to receive questions and concerns, check failed reach, identify information gaps and assess harmful false claims. Prioritize by safety/action impact, audience reach and evidence; repeating a low-reach false claim can amplify it. Verify before correction. Correct the record promptly through routes reaching the affected audience; state the change and current instruction. Preserve the exact earlier message, correction, authorization, time and where sent. Do not silently erase evidence, equate criticism with misinformation, or disclose protected information while rebutting a claim. A generic social-media monitoring plan is not permission to activate collection.

## C09 Sensitive events and applicable duties

Prepare scoped message cards for the relevant assessed crises, including injury/death/missing person, safeguarding allegations, leader or staff conduct, financial concerns, cyber/privacy incidents, public-health events and service interruption. Include start triggers, competent authority, independent route around a conflicted leader, privacy/identity checks, care, required notices, permitted content and next update. Separate immediate safety, external statutory/contractual notification, internal governance, family communication and public messaging. Verify applicable jurisdictions, recipients, deadlines, insurer/contract terms, retention and professional advice. Do not automatically apply HIPAA, a school/airport duty, ADA coverage/exemption or another sector's authority to every organization. No reputation or clearance process may suppress a required report or erase evidence.

## C10 Care recovery and ending the update cycle

Provide practical, private help routes and appropriate pastoral/community or qualified professional support without promising clinical services or confidentiality beyond actual authority. Maintain consistent updates on service status, alternative arrangements, residual risk and next decisions. Name who may issue an all clear, reopening or closure message, on what verified basis and in coordination with competent authorities. Do not equate reopening with complete recovery. Continue needed family care and correction, explain verified changes, preserve records, review actual performance and assign lessons/corrective actions with retest.

## C11 Reusable plan tools

Provide a short first-action route; per-role cards with triggers, limits, backup and accepted handoff; first/holding/update/correction/recovery message forms; an audience/channel and backup map; restricted contact directory; fact and message logs; approval/delegation record; shift handoff; legal duty map; and test/maintenance records. Use the existing stable finding/action/decision identities and one governed action record. Do not multiply repetitive annexes or duplicate registers. Keep detailed analysis and restricted information outside the simple action route while preserving clear locators.

## C12 Test acceptance and maintenance

Define scenario-specific acceptance measures before testing: locate the correct step, explain it in the user's own words, choose the safe action, meet the justified time, reach intended groups, operate a backup, correct an error and transfer an open task to an accepting owner. Agree target values with competent owners using the assessment; do not invent universal response times. Mark test traffic and isolate live alert lists. Retain participants, observer method, conditions, actual results, limitations, fixes and retests. Assistant walkthroughs and software checks are not actual audience, partner or specialist validation.

Keep review schedule, document/version owner, records, access/rights controls and change triggers aligned with P12. Topic coverage, readability, legal applicability, source qualification, exact-file quality, human plan approval and tested readiness are separate claim gates. Policy merge does not populate a Member plan, deliver a message, authorize service, prove legal compliance or establish universal superiority.
