# Salus analytical assumptions-to-action standard

**Status:** Approved member-neutral method under SAL-D055, 28 September 2026. **Owner:** Salus Solutions. **Handling:** Salus Internal Confidential. **Scope:** Every authorized SalINTEL™, SalTHIRA™, SalOPS™ and SalCAPA™ package at Levels 1–3; Protection Advisor uses only bounded educational signals. Apply with the Program Charter, Decision Register, SAL-D025, SAL-D027, SAL-D043, SAL-D050–D054, the approved product standards and Protected Member controls. This standard defines analyst judgment and report expression; it does not issue Member findings, authorize operations, change a live questionnaire, revise a Word template or deploy software.

## Purpose and required outcome

Salus shall not stop after asking questions, gathering records and listing uncertainty. For each material decision, the qualified analyst must form a reasoned **working assumption** when evidence is incomplete, analyze what follows if it is true or false, and turn that analysis into a specific recommendation that the Protected Member can evaluate and act on. A working assumption is an explicit, testable basis for a decision under uncertainty. It is not a finding, a legal conclusion, a probability invented from thin evidence, or permission to bypass missing authority.

Each material recommendation appears in **two linked forms**:

1. A plain-language **narrative** explaining the condition or strength, supporting evidence, assumption and alternative explanation, consequence, causal mechanism, options and why Salus recommends this course at the represented level.
2. An **action checklist** naming concrete steps, owner and approving authority, trigger or sequence, deadline or decision window, required resources and dependencies, stop/safety condition, proof of completion, and the evidence that would validate, revise or retire the assumption.

Both forms use the same recommendation ID and agree on scope, priority, uncertainty and authorization state. A checklist without analysis is not a complete recommendation; a narrative without operational steps is not an actionable one.

## Analyst reasoning chain

Use stable identifiers so a reviewer can follow:

`question → source/claim → qualified finding and contradiction → working assumption → alternative/falsifier → scenario/affected objective → capability target and current evidence → gap or strength → option comparison → recommendation narrative → action checklist → Member decision → proof/review`.

For each assumption record:

- **Basis:** observed or reported signal, source/date, quality, search boundary, contrary evidence, jurisdiction and affected Member objective.
- **Statement:** one precise, testable proposition with geography, period and operating conditions. Avoid labeling a person, vendor or organization as a threat on speculation.
- **Confidence and stakes:** bounded confidence description and what changes if wrong; separate likelihood, consequence and urgency where evidence permits. Do not turn a confidence label into a fabricated statistic.
- **Alternatives:** at least one plausible alternative explanation or an explicit reason none is decision-relevant; note the favorable case as well as the adverse case.
- **Validation:** the least burdensome lawful evidence or observation that would change the decision, owner, due point, specialist trigger and next-level handoff.
- **Interim posture:** what can responsibly be recommended now while validation is pending, including no action, reversible precaution or urgent referral when appropriate.

An assumption may support a **conditional** recommendation when waiting for certainty would forfeit a meaningful opportunity or raise exposure. Phrase the condition and decision gate plainly: “If [test/condition], [authorized action]; otherwise [alternative/reassessment].” For high-consequence action, preserve competent professional review, legal authority, safety and Member approval. Missing evidence is not proof of noncompliance or a finding of wrongdoing.

## Specific recommendation rule

Every material recommendation has a named problem or opportunity and an observable result. Compare no action and at least one feasible alternative when the choice is consequential. Explain why the proposed intervention changes the mechanism, what strength it preserves, who bears cost or burden, what linked systems it depends on, what it may worsen, and how success and residual risk will be checked. State whether the item is **suggested**, **Member-selected**, **separately authorized**, **implemented**, **validated**, **deferred** or **rejected**. Only the appropriate Member/Salus authorities can move an item to an operational state.

A checklist line starts with an action verb and describes one verifiable task. It includes a deliverable or observation, owner, due window, prerequisite and evidence of completion. Avoid “improve preparedness,” “coordinate,” “consider security,” and similar language unless the exact meeting, decision, record, test and result are stated. Do not invent a specific vendor, dollar value, deadline, travel time or capacity when the evidence cannot support it; give a bounded decision window or “to be set by the authorized owner” and identify the needed input. For urgent life-safety concerns, provide proportionate immediate protective guidance and appropriate emergency referral, while clearly separating it from an unverified allegation or a paid service offer.

## Required paired report pattern

### Recommendation [ID] — [specific outcome] — [priority and represented level]

**Narrative.** In [scope and time], [verified/reported condition and strength] matters because [causal pathway to objective or capability]. Salus assumes [testable proposition, confidence and basis], while [alternative/contrary evidence] remains possible. If the assumption holds, [consequence/target/gap]; if it does not, [decision change]. Of [no action and feasible options], Salus recommends [specific course] because [expected benefit, constraints, side effects and residual risk]. [Authorized decision owner] should decide [decision/window]. [Named evidence or test] will confirm or revise this recommendation at [Level 1/2/3 boundary].

| Action checklist | Owner / authority | Timing or trigger | Dependency / stop condition | Proof and review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ☐ [Concrete verifiable step 1] | [Named role; approval status] | [Decision window] | [Prerequisite and safety/legal condition] | [Record, observed result and reviewer] |
| ☐ [Concrete verifiable step 2] | [Named role; approval status] | [Sequence or trigger] | [Resource/partner and fallback] | [Test/acceptance and recheck trigger] |

Tailor the length to the decision. The table is a report presentation pattern, not an instruction to expose restricted sources or Salus scoring logic. Include a separate **assumptions to validate** row when it would otherwise be lost among tasks.

## Level-specific depth and product roles

| Level | Analyst assumption and action standard |
|---|---|
| **1 Initial Pass** | Produce a qualified, source-bound working assumption for each priority decision where evidence is incomplete. Recommend immediate proportionate decisions, reversible precautions, targeted validation and specific Level 2 questions. The same-level SalTHIRA™ and SalOPS™ analysis must still produce useful options; do not defer all recommendations merely because field validation is pending. Label what is untested and what would change the recommendation. |
| **2 Focused Assessment** | Test selected Level 1 assumptions using authorized records, interviews, partner confirmation, observation or qualified specialist review. Revise scenario, target, option and checklist in the existing report lineage; explain any changed conclusion. |
| **3 Deep Dive** | Test approved compound dependencies, adverse effects, stress conditions and sustained performance. Compare sensitivity and alternatives, record residual uncertainty and monitoring triggers, and require the relevant human decision/release gates. |

- **SalINTEL™** teaches why an issue matters, qualifies evidence and contradictions, states any decision-changing working assumption and its validation request, and prepares the scenario/capability handoff. It may recommend immediate lawful evidence-preserving or protective steps within scope, but does not issue operational orders.
- **SalTHIRA™** uses those assumptions transparently in its existing six steps. It tests how each affects scenario selection, consequence, targets, capability gaps, ranked priorities and treatment options across the five mission areas and applicable 32 core capabilities. Its BLUF carries the highest-priority narrative and paired checklist recommendations for same-level SalOPS™ consideration.
- **SalOPS™** translates approved priorities into feasible action options and, only when separately authorized, plans/tasks. Each option has the narrative and checklist pair plus authority, safety, cost/resource and validation gates. A recommendation is not authorization to perform.
- **SalCAPA™** reconciles the same-level report chain into a concise decision narrative and master action checklist. It shows accepted/deferred choices, owner, resource, evidence, proof, residual risk and the next-level validation choice. Do not multiply contradictory checklists across reports.
- **Protection Advisor** may state an educational hypothesis based on consented answers and suggest a voluntary Level 1 assessment or separately scoped service. Anonymous Part 1 does not make a person-level or site-specific determination, and the Advisor does not bypass the four-product analytical chain.

## Example of the distinction

*Hypothetical illustration, not a finding about any Protected Member:* Existing records describe two exit gates, but do not establish whether both can be opened without grid power. A Level 1 analyst may assume simultaneous power loss could leave only one usable exit. The favorable alternative is that a manual release works and staff know how to use it. The recommendation narrative explains how a single exit could delay evacuation or responder access and why a witnessed, safe gate-opening test is preferable to treating the map as proof. The paired checklist assigns an authorized facilities owner to inventory the releases, test both under an agreed safe condition with a witness, record opening time and failure mode, update the evacuation instruction and request a focused Level 2 review if a gate cannot be opened reliably. It does **not** report that a gate has failed or direct an unsafe test.

## Quality and release gate

The SAL-D062 operating chain makes the recommended decision, authority, service state and observed proof fields mandatory for each material item. Reuse one stable ID across research, product reports, service record and SalCAPA™; preserve a falsifier and next review trigger. A strong narrative must make the mechanism comprehensible to the Protected Member, while the paired checklist must make the authorized next action and test possible. Apply the full shared standard in `SALUS_ENTERPRISE_DECISION_TO_PROOF_OPERATING_STANDARD.md` and keep the item at its true evidence/authorization state.

Before a report or recommendation is issued, a qualified analyst and reviewer check that each material assumption has a source and alternative, each recommendation is causally justified, and its narrative and checklist match. Confirm that the action is specific enough to carry out, proportional to evidence, lawful and within scope; that affected people, linked systems, unintended effects, funding and competing resources were considered; and that the validation trigger can actually be observed. Apply Member isolation, confidentiality, versioned evidence and SAL-D043 visual/report QA. A working assumption may remain in a clearly marked working report; a final Member decision or implemented task requires the applicable human approvals and governed release.

Salus may describe this evidence-to-judgment-to-action approach as its method in proposals and education. Do not assert that no other methodology does this or make an unverified market superiority claim.
