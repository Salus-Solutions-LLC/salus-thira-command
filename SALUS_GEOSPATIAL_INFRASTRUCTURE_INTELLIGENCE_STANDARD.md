# Salus geospatial and infrastructure intelligence standard

**Authority:** SAL-D056, 28 September 2026. **Scope:** Member-neutral method for Protection Advisor and SalCAPA™, SalINTEL™, SalTHIRA™, and SalOPS™ at Levels 1–3. A map is evidence with a date, scale, coverage and purpose; it is not proof of current condition, ownership, operability or a service commitment.

## Decision purpose and collection boundary

For each Protected Member, identify how location, terrain, waterways, infrastructure and regional activity could affect people, essential functions, the five mission areas and applicable core capabilities. Begin with a bounded area of interest (AOI), asset/function question, planning horizon and sensitivity classification. Confirm the Member's site boundary and critical asset locations through an authorized source before asserting a distance, overlap or exposure. Record the search area and sources actually reviewed. No internet search can establish that every relevant map or hazard has been found.

Salus may collect lawful public, non-sensitive layers within the authorized assessment. Do not publish exact private gates, alternate exits, access credentials, critical equipment locations, resident locations, security coverage or exploitable dependency diagrams in an ordinary client copy. Keep restricted working overlays within the Member engagement boundary and share only with a demonstrated role and purpose. Consult utility owners and qualified engineers before construction or excavation; public pipeline/electric maps are screening aids only.

## Minimum layer screen and authoritative leads

| Layer | Decision questions | Primary public lead and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Recent and historical imagery | Land-cover change, damaged crossings, fire fuel, erosion, nearby development, access changes | USDA FSA NAIP aerial imagery and USGS Landsat; note acquisition date, resolution, season and cloud cover. NAIP is aerial, not satellite. A picture does not verify structural safety or current conditions. |
| Elevation, slope and drainage | Flow paths, low points, steep or unstable approaches, communications sight lines | USGS 3DEP and 3D Hydrography; compare model resolution with the asset. Field survey and engineering review govern individual culverts, dams and embankments. |
| Flood and waterways | Creeks, crossings, floodplain, upstream/downstream dependency, evacuation timing | FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer and USGS hydrography/gauges where available. Effective flood maps do not describe every flood mechanism or prove a crossing passable today. |
| Roads, bridges and access | Primary/alternate routes, chokepoints, bridge load, response travel and regional competition | TxDOT road/bridge inventory and local road authorities. Obtain Member private-road, gate and crossing records separately; a mapped road does not prove public access, load capacity or passability. |
| Power and communications | Grid supply, backup endurance, common-mode dependency, service restoration | EIA public transmission/generation layers and utility/service-provider evidence. Public maps do not prove a particular distribution feeder, switch, outage history, priority or redundancy. |
| Oil, gas and hazardous-liquid pipelines | Easements, nearby corridors, emergency coordination and excavation planning | Texas Railroad Commission GIS and PHMSA NPMS. Public NPMS has approximate placement and omits some pipeline classes; confirm with operator and 811. Do not use it to locate a line for digging. |
| Rail, airports and aviation | Transport incident scenarios, crossing/access disruption and air operations | TxDOT rail maps, FAA airport data and local operator/authority records. Proximity alone does not establish traffic or accident likelihood. |
| Regulated facilities and hazardous material flow | Possible industrial release, transport corridor, evacuation and responder demands | EPA ECHO for regulated facilities and PHMSA commodity-flow statistics. Aggregate commodity data do not identify a specific road/rail route, load, frequency or actual exposure. Obtain a route-specific study and local responder validation when decision-changing. |
| Other applicable layers | Wildfire fuel/response, drought, land use, wells/water quality, health access, shelters, livestock/exotics and mutual aid | Use current competent federal, Texas, county, local and owner/operator sources; record applicability and exclusions. A protected or security-sensitive layer requires a restricted handling decision. |

Primary starting points: [USGS National Map](https://www.usgs.gov/the-national-map-data-delivery/gis-data-download), [USGS 3D Hydrography](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-hydrography-program/access-3dhp-data-products), [USDA FSA imagery](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/online-services/fsa-online-data-resources), [FEMA flood maps](https://www.fema.gov/flood-maps), [TxDOT road inventory](https://www.txdot.gov/data-maps/roadway-inventory.html), [TxDOT rail maps](https://www.txdot.gov/discover/rail/railroad-maps.html), [Texas RRC GIS](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/gis-viewer/), [PHMSA NPMS](https://www.npms.phmsa.dot.gov/About.aspx), [EPA ECHO](https://echo.epa.gov/), [PHMSA commodity-flow guidance](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/regulatory-compliance/phmsa-guidance/hazardous-materials-commodity-flow-data-and-analysis), [EIA public energy data](https://www.eia.gov/tools/Faqs/faq.php?id=567&t=1). Verify each dataset's current edition, coverage, access rights and use restrictions when applied.

## Evidence and analysis sequence

1. Define AOI, assets/functions, questions, time window, coordinate reference, disclosure tier and owner. Record whether a boundary is confirmed, approximate or absent.
2. Inventory source, publisher, layer name/version, acquisition and access dates, spatial/temporal resolution, legend, coordinate system, completeness, license, update cadence and access restriction. Save a reproducible pointer or permitted snapshot; retain provenance without making a second authoritative source copy.
3. Align layers and dates. Compare imagery from at least two dates when change matters. Note seasonal effects, occlusion, imagery artifacts, map-generalization and positional uncertainty. Use field and owner records to challenge map inference.
4. Screen direct and cascading exposure: people, animals, access, power, water, communications, medical response, fuel, supply, environmental consequence, insurance/recovery and competing public resources. Test regional simultaneous disruption and the failure of a preferred alternate route or backup.
5. Label each claim **verified observation, reported condition, modelled estimate, analytical assumption, contradictory evidence, missing evidence or unmodelled possibility**. Do not turn an apparent overlap into an asserted violation or a quantitative probability without competent analysis.
6. Link source IDs to scenario, mission area, capability, target, current capacity, gap, no-action and options. Explain causal pathway and counterexample. State what observation would change the ranking.
7. For each material finding, give the Member a concise explanation plus a specific narrative recommendation and matching checklist under SAL-D055. Record owner, authority, timing, dependency, cost range only if supported, safe-stop condition, proof and next review. Send specialist, utility and emergency-service questions to qualified counterparts.

## Levels and product handoff

| Product | Level 1 Initial Pass | Level 2 Focused Assessment | Level 3 Deep Dive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protection Advisor | Minimal educational location/dependency prompts; no upload of detailed security maps in anonymous Part 1. | Not a stand-alone entry level; use an authenticated, consented referral to a qualified package. | No direct entry. |
| SalINTEL™ | Public-layer screen and Member map inventory; visible provenance, contradictions, restricted overlays and priority missing data. | Validate selected routes, assets and dependencies with owner records, field observation and competent specialists. | Approved integrated spatial model, temporal change and compound interdependency analysis with sensitivity tests. |
| SalTHIRA™ | Carry qualified map evidence and uncertainty through all six steps; rank plausible geographic consequences and provisional capability targets/gaps. | Recalculate selected targets/gaps using validated exposure, route, restoration and responder inputs. | Test approved compound outages, infrastructure cascade, climate/land-use change and uncertainty ranges. |
| SalOPS™ | Give specific feasible protective, monitoring, planning and verification options; do not treat maps as work authorization. | Develop scoped route, continuity, engineering or partner-validation tasks with acceptance proof. | Coordinate approved implementation, exercises, resource staging and retest across systems. |
| SalCAPA™ | Reconcile the same-level three reports into a location-aware decision summary without publishing exploitable detail. | Document validated changes, owner decisions, funding and next-level prerequisites. | Integrate cross-system treatment, residual risk, monitoring and governed lessons. |

Every report marks its represented level in front matter, executive summary and material map findings. It says when higher-resolution, owner, field or specialist evidence would change a decision. Each level depends on a qualified prior package under SAL-D032. The geospatial method does not create a separate report or automatic upgrade.

## Member evidence request and report treatment

After Level 1 authorization, request only what resolves a named decision: confirmed property/AOI boundary; current private road, gate, crossing, bridge, drainage and dam asset inventory; permitted emergency route maps; utility/service and outage information; backup power/fuel/well/water arrangements; relevant easements; historical incident closures; land-use/construction plans; and available responder/owner validation. Ask for sensitive utility and access material through a restricted route and record rights. Distinguish an absent map from an absent capability. Keep a source-to-map-to-assumption-to-action ID chain and separate restricted map annex when needed.

Before issue, check layer dates, projection, legend, scale, text/map consistency, source rights, access permissions, accessibility/alternative text, accurate labels and SAL-D043 Word/PDF/print rendering. Review active and issued reports under SAL-D027; no silent replacement or duplicate governed report.
