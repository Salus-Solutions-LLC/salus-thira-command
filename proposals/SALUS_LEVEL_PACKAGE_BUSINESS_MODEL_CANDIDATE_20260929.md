## The proposed service structure in one view

**First, learn and prepare.** Protection Advisor Part 1 explains why an assessment may help. Optional Part 2 identifies the operation the prospective Protected Member wants assessed, suggests a provisional scope band and explains what to prepare. Neither part is a paid assessment or a verified finding.

**Next, choose the boundary.** Band A covers one defined site or function with one integrated decision and action plan. Band B covers two named sites or one site/function whose materially different operations require distinct findings and a shared-versus-specific comparison. Band C covers a defined group of sites or functions needing group-wide priority choices. Salus confirms the band from the analysis required, not from customer type, revenue, headcount or address count alone.

**Then, choose how deeply to examine that same boundary.** Level 1 establishes a useful baseline and action plan. Level 2 adds visits, interviews and targeted validation. Level 3 adds approved specialist work, testing or assurance for a named decision. A completed Level 1 precedes Level 2; Level 2 precedes Level 3. A newly added site or function first needs its own qualified Level 1 coverage. Each paid package produces four connected SalINTEL™, SalTHIRA™, SalOPS™ and SalCAPA™ reports.

**Name the path plainly.** The first paid engagement is a **Level 1 SalCAPA™ package**. An eligible simple boundary is **Level 1A Access**; a more involved integrated unit is **Level 1A Standard**; separate comparative or group scopes use B or C. Future report titles pair the Protected Member name, one of the four product names and the exact path, for example `Example Child Care - SalTHIRA Level 1A Access`. The cover and executive summary name the covered decision unit and exclusions. Protection Advisor Part 2 is a provisional **scope and readiness profile**, not the first report. Previously issued report names retain their history.

**What Salus delivers at every paid level:** a defensible, trackable benchmark of the covered operation's current capability against a stated scenario target; prioritized recommendations that explain the evidence, consequence, effort and reason for the order; and clear decision support stating what the Protected Member can approve now, who owns the action, what it may cost, what proof would show progress and when to reassess. Each benchmark records its source, date, assumptions, confidence and limits so later levels can confirm or revise it. It is a comparison to an explicit capability target, not an unsupported claim that the Member is above or below industry peers. A peer comparison is made only when a suitable, current and comparable source is identified and its limits are shown.

### Choose a package in two questions

**1. What must this purchase cover?** **A** means one site or function with one combined set of findings and actions. **B** means two named sites or different operations that need their own findings and a comparison. **C** means a named group for which leadership must choose priorities across units. **2. How much proof is needed now?** **Level 1** uses records, public sources and remote clarification to establish a first decision. **Level 2** visits and interviews people to check the Level 1 questions that could change that decision. **Level 3** adds a quoted specialist, test or exercise module for a consequential decision. Access is the $1,500 Level 1A option only when its stated one-site, one-activity boundary fits. A Member can implement a Level 1 action without buying Level 2.

After the test, its measured result can update the same decision record. The four reports are included in the assessment price. Designing or running the notification test for the Member is a separately authorized SalOPS service.

Implement the selected **$1,500 Level 1A Access price** and settle a commercially deliverable boundary for each *integrated* level while retaining the required SalINTEL™ → SalTHIRA™ → SalOPS™ → SalCAPA™ sequence, all six SalTHIRA steps, the five mission areas and applicability screen for 32 core capabilities. ## Affordable entry strategy

The earlier $12,000–$20,000 one-site Level 1 candidate is **too high as a universal starting price**. It describes a high-touch, research-heavy engagement, not what every small Protected Member can buy. A single small daycare may value a $1,500 start; a large company may reasonably pay $15,000 for a bounded office or warehouse Level 1 if the scope and evidence justify it. Neither customer should receive a fictitious enterprise-wide conclusion. Salus should compete on a useful first decision, transparent boundaries, speed, continuity of evidence and access to implementation help—not on a thick report or an unsupported claim of comprehensive safety.

Offer **Level 1A Access at $1,500** for an eligible, one-site, one-operation Member: short digital intake; an automated official/local source pack; a bounded Member document packet; a recorded 32-capability applicability screen and six SalTHIRA steps; two decision-driving scenarios; a short clarification; four concise, distinct reports produced from one evidence/decision graph; one human analyst/reviewer determination; and a prioritized action checklist with owner, timing, proof and next-level questions. Covers and summaries must state that it is Level 1A Access, the included site/function, the date and the limits. This is the selected price and proposed exact product boundary, not a claim that current SalCAPA software already delivers it. Child-care duties, staff/child safety, emergency communications, reunification and jurisdiction-specific licensing still need competent screening; an automated check must not assert compliance or substitute for an inspection.

The Access eligibility gate is **operational simplicity and available evidence**, not low revenue or small headcount alone: one address and decision owner, one main activity, a short usable intake, no complex multi-employer or multi-jurisdiction scope, no unresolved material contradiction needing extensive original-source pursuit, and no promised field validation. A human screener may find an important hazard or legal question and route it to a qualified specialist, public resource, separately priced work or an urgent action. Do not downgrade the safety finding to keep the price. If the gate fails, explain why, define a suitable Level 1 scope and quote, and identify any immediately useful public resource; no bait-and-switch.

**SalCAPA production design:** reuse governed sector question packs, official-source indexes and local-hazard data; ingest the Member's authorized documents once; extract dates and assertions with provenance; detect contradictions and missing evidence; populate SalINTEL, SalTHIRA, SalOPS and SalCAPA from stable decision IDs; generate a short narrative plus action checklist; and run automatic style, link, footer, blank-page, privacy and cross-report checks. An analyst must review source-to-claim links, applicability, high-consequence conclusions, assumptions, recommendations and exclusions before issue. Measure actual retrieval, interpretation, correction and review time. NIST's AI risk framework calls for documented roles, review and validation; automation is a cost lever only when output quality is demonstrated. [NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/); [NIST Small Business Quick-Start Guides](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber/quick-start-guides).

At the selected **$1,500** fee and an illustrative **40% contribution margin**, the maximum variable cost would be **$900**. If acquisition and platform/data/payment expense consume **$300**, **$600** remains for human delivery—about **5 hours at an assumed fully loaded $120 per hour**. These are planning assumptions, not observed Salus costs or a limit on required review. Record actual cost per package, source load, human hours, QA failures, rework, support burden, customer comprehension and later uptake. If effort exceeds the planning case, improve automation, workflow or funding and review the scope and economics openly; preserve the qualified four-product Member promise and the approved $1,500 price for eligible signed work. [SBA break-even and contribution-margin method](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/calculate-your-startup-costs/break-even-point); [FTC advertising guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business).

## Education and market entry

Many potential Members have never purchased an integrated assessment and may not know what decision it helps them make. Explain the offer through familiar questions: **What could interrupt us? Who decides? What can we do now? How would we know it worked?** Show a one-page sample with a real-world style action, owner and proof requirement, plus a plain explanation of the four Salus reports. Explain the three assessment levels and scoped service profiles without making the first purchase feel like a commitment to all three. **Protection Advisor Part 1 builds awareness and teaches the value of an assessment. Part 2 helps the prospective Member identify the facility/function, purpose and information needed to start a Level 1 SalCAPA engagement.** Its output should be a plain-language readiness and next-step summary, clearly distinct from SalINTEL, SalTHIRA, SalOPS or SalCAPA findings. Its handoff should tell the Member what Level 1 will answer, what will happen next, the likely scope/price path and a short, staged list of records to prepare after agreement. With appropriate consent and source controls, carry relevant answers forward so the Member does not re-enter them; allow a save-and-resume path without duplicate submissions. Identify by role the prospective contracting decision-maker and records coordinator, then verify their authority before agreement or transfer. Missing contacts are readiness tasks rather than risk findings.

SalCAPA's proposed market advantage is **lower marginal production cost with maintained or improved evidence quality**: reusable source indexes, sector logic, automation of provenance and document production, and qualified human decisions. That is a proposition to validate, not a current performance claim. Compare early Access packages with a human-reviewed benchmark for source accuracy, material omitted issues, cross-report consistency, recommendation usefulness, time to issue and cost. Sample unsuccessful and high-consequence cases, not only easy ones. Customer-facing claims about speed and superior quality need measured support; the selected $1,500 price needs an exact, honest scope beside it. The most meaningful disruption would be making a useful first decision affordable to previously unserved small organizations and creating a trusted route to implementation—not merely shortening analyst time. ## Insurance value for Protected Members

There is **no verified general premium discount merely for being a Salus Protected Member**. A carrier, its filed rating plan, the policy line, jurisdiction and the Member's actual risk controls determine whether a credit or better terms are available. Texas workers' compensation guidance identifies experience modifiers and optional schedule debits/credits based on an employer's characteristics; criteria differ by carrier. NAIC notes documented safety practices may support lower commercial-auto risk assessments. A separate Texas small-employer workers' compensation discount for qualifying loss history must not be represented as a Salus-created saving. [Texas workers' compensation rate guide](https://agate.tdi.texas.gov/wc/regulation/rcomp.html); [NAIC small-business insurance guide](https://content.naic.org/consumer/small-business.htm); [Texas small-employer rule](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/rules/2021/documents/b1444rfiling2.pdf).

Design an optional **insurance readiness pathway** across all levels: (1) identify current policies, limits, deductibles, renewal date and broker/carrier contacts with Member consent; (2) compare insurer/loss-control recommendations and claims patterns to Salus findings; (3) recommend and document actual corrective actions; (4) assemble evidence the Member may give its licensed broker or carrier, including dated photographs, training and exercise records, maintenance/inspection proof and current plans; (5) ask the broker or carrier in writing which specific credits, coverage options, deductibles, conditions or eligibility changes, if any, the implemented measures may support; (6) verify the issued quote or renewal against the prior policy on like-for-like coverage; and (7) record accepted, denied, pending and not-applicable outcomes. The insurer or broker determines coverage and premium; a Salus report is not certification of a control or a promise of savings. Texas workers' compensation carriers must provide certain accident-prevention services, which Salus should help Members use rather than charge to duplicate. [Texas carrier safety resources](https://tdi.texas.gov/wc/safety/reswkrscompins.html).

Potential Member value includes **premium reduction, avoided surcharge, improved insurability or available limits, more favorable deductibles/terms, fewer losses and less interruption**. Do not add these into a single claimed savings figure or attribute a change to Salus without a matched baseline and carrier confirmation. Salus can explore insurer or broker partnerships to recognize verified mitigation, but only after identifying the carrier, product, state, eligibility, evidence, filed/authorized credit mechanism and legal/commercial relationship. Membership alone cannot be advertised as a discount; counsel and licensed insurance professionals must review any referral, group-purchase or incentive arrangement before launch. Texas commercial-property rate filings and insurance inducement rules make that review material. [Texas commercial-property filing checklist](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/commercial/pcckprop.html); [Texas Insurance Code Chapter 1806](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/pdf/IN.1806.pdf).

### Proposed band-assignment decision rule

1. **Test Access eligibility first.** One site/function, one primary activity and decision owner, usable bounded intake and no promised fieldwork may fit Level 1A Access. A material question still receives a qualified disposition; it is never ignored to preserve the price.
2. **Use A Standard when one integrated decision remains.** More records, interviews by role, scenarios or regulatory screening can justify A Standard, but do not by themselves create a B comparison. If the justified A work exceeds the candidate price range, quote and approve a tailored A scope rather than relabeling it B without B work.
3. **Use B when distinct findings must be compared.** Two named sites, or materially different operating profiles within one site, require separate conclusions and a shared-versus-specific synthesis of controls, dependencies and actions. The quote names the compared profiles and the decision the comparison supports. A second address alone does not prove commonality; review its actual operations before using this profile.
4. **Use C when leadership needs group priorities.** A named group of sites/functions, shared resource allocation and group-wide tradeoffs require a defined portfolio slice, with inclusion, exclusion and any sampling stated. Do not call one complicated activity a portfolio merely to reach a higher price.

Record the actual work driver, compared units or integrated decision, source assumptions, Member-facing reason, reviewer and any exception in the scope record. Part 2 may suggest a band; the Salus scoping reviewer confirms it. Revenue, headcount, file count, regulation and customer prominence are context or effort signals, not automatic B/C triggers. A written override must explain why the chosen band produces a different Member deliverable and fee. The signed scope governs; a later change requires a written change before extra work or charge.

### Five-mission-area offer rule and concrete examples

Each SalOPS finding should name the **capability gap first**, then the service and result: prevention (reduce the chance of an incident), protection (shield people/assets), mitigation (reduce consequences), response (act during disruption) and recovery (restore functions). For example, a weak visitor/vendor control can lead to an approved access procedure and role training, with a controlled-entry observation as proof; an untested evacuation notification can lead to a message tree, alternate-channel test and timed acknowledgement result; a single-point supplier can lead to a continuity alternate and measured restoration exercise. The qualified analyst records applicability, urgency, Member-owned option, public/partner option, Salus option, prerequisites, price status, licensing/capacity and acceptance test. A Level 1 recommendation may lead directly to a separately authorized SalOPS service without buying Level 2; higher-level assessment is proposed only when new proof would change a consequential decision. Any live response or staffed protection needs its own authority and operational controls regardless of assessment level.
