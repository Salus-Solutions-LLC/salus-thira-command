# Salus organizational health, leadership and command assessment method

**Status:** Approved member-neutral enterprise method by Marc, 28 September 2026 (SAL-D051); not a Protected Member finding, issued report, legal opinion or deployed workflow.  
**Date:** 28 September 2026.  
**Applies to:** SalCAPA™, SalINTEL™, SalTHIRA™ and SalOPS™ at Levels 1, 2 and 3.  
**Authority:** The Salus Program Charter, Decision Register, approved product standards and six-step SalTHIRA method control if this proposal conflicts with them. No source below automatically creates a duty for a Protected Member.

## 1. Purpose and assessment question

Determine whether a Protected Member can make legitimate, timely, informed decisions; turn them into understood action; detect when circumstances or assumptions change; maintain essential operations and ethical controls; and recover performance under stress. Report demonstrable strengths as well as weaknesses. A written chart or policy is evidence of design, not proof that it works.

The assessment asks five linked questions: **Who may decide? On what information and within what limits? How does the decision reach and guide the people doing the work? How does the organization notice and correct divergence? What happens when leaders, people, systems or outside support are unavailable?**

Use the method for corporations, nonprofits, HOAs, ranches, volunteer bodies, public entities and mixed contractor ecosystems. First determine the entity's legal form and actual governing instruments. Do not import corporate board, military command or public-agency requirements without checking applicability.

## 2. Concepts the analyst explains to the Member

- **Governance** sets purpose, risk appetite, oversight, accountability and the boundary between governing body and management. It includes conflicts, finances, ethics and stakeholder voice.
- **Command and coordination** specify who has authority to direct whom, and how peers, contractors and public responders coordinate. Ordinary management, legal emergency authority and incident command may differ.
- **Decision rights** identify the decision owner, required consultation, approval threshold, time limit, escalation trigger, record and review. Accountability cannot be delegated away merely by transferring a task.
- **Delegation** is written authority to perform specified functions within limits. **Succession** identifies who assumes a position when the incumbent cannot serve. Test both separately.
- **Promulgation** is the full passage from approving a rule or decision through versioned publication, receipt, understanding, training, accessible use, reinforcement, and withdrawal of old versions. Sending an email proves transmission, not adoption.
- **Dynamic leadership** changes tempo, team structure and decision placement as conditions change while preserving purpose, lawful authority, safeguards and a common operating picture. Distributed initiative needs explicit boundaries, competence, trust and review.
- **Organizational pattern recognition** is disciplined detection of recurring bottlenecks, weak signals, workarounds, concentration of decisions, inconsistent outcomes, near misses, and changes in the outside environment. It requires denominators and competing explanations, not anecdotal labeling of people.
- **Organizational health** is the combined effectiveness, integrity and resilience of these mechanisms in ordinary work and disruption. A popular leader or attractive chart alone is insufficient evidence.

## 3. Twelve diagnostic domains

Every Level 1 screen covers all domains. At higher levels, scale evidence and testing to materiality.

| Domain | What to establish | Strength indicators | Concern indicators |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Purpose and governance | Mission, objectives, governing instruments, board/management boundary, stakeholder duties | Priorities and oversight are clear, lawful and reviewed | Conflicting mandates, shadow governance, unmanaged conflicts |
| 2. Authority and succession | Legal and practical decision rights, thresholds, emergency powers, acting officials | Written limits, accessible records, trained alternates | Single person controls all decisions, unclear emergency spending or succession |
| 3. Decision quality | Inputs, uncertainty, options, dissent, risk acceptance, decision logs | Timely options and assumptions recorded; dissent considered | Decisions rely on habit, rank or incomplete data; repeated reversals |
| 4. Process and controls | Essential work, process owners, handoffs, segregation, exceptions, vendor oversight | Critical workflows repeat reliably with proportionate controls | Bottlenecks, undocumented workarounds, no check on sensitive transactions |
| 5. Leadership and culture | Competence, behavior, accountability, trust, speaking up, conflict resolution | People can raise concerns; leaders act and explain | Retaliation fears, hidden errors, dominance by informal figures |
| 6. Promulgation and change | Policy lifecycle, audience, accessibility, acknowledgement, training, retirement | Current direction is found, understood and practiced | Multiple versions, untrained roles, announcement without follow-through |
| 7. Information and sensemaking | Signal collection, quality, escalation, common picture, external scanning | Weak signals reach decision makers with context | Bad news filtered out; “no incidents” assumed to mean no risk |
| 8. Coordination and communications | Internal, Member, contractor, public authority and crisis channels | Contact routes, message authority and feedback loops work | Contradictory messages, unverified receipt, single-channel failure |
| 9. People, capacity and dependencies | Staffing, volunteers, fatigue, skills, suppliers, mutual aid | Capability and reserves match demand; agreements tested | Hidden labor dependence, unqualified alternates, unrealistic mutual aid |
| 10. Ethics, compliance and financial stewardship | Applicable duties, procurement, conflicts, anti-fraud, records, insurance conditions | Legal review, independent checks and defensible records | Self-approval, informal deals, untracked exposure or stale compliance assumptions |
| 11. Continuity and incident transition | Essential functions, triggers, command establishment/transfer, recovery governance | Succession, delegations, alternate communications and exercises work | Plan names unavailable people or powers; incident-to-recovery handoff missing |
| 12. Learning and adaptation | Performance measures, near misses, after-action review, corrective-action closure | Changes are tested and sustained; effective local practice is retained | Same issue recurs; recommendations close on paper only |

The analyst also maps cross-cutting effects on the five mission areas and 32 core capabilities without inventing a new FEMA capability or claiming all 32 apply equally to every Member.

## 4. Evidence and fieldwork sequence

1. **Define the Member and boundary.** Record legal entity, locations, affiliates, contractors, volunteer groups, governing instruments, jurisdictions, essential services, major stakeholders, assessment period, and exclusions. Confirm who can supply records and who can approve findings.
2. **Map formal authority.** Review charter/bylaws, contracts, employment/volunteer arrangements, board resolutions, delegations, spending limits, insurance requirements, incident plans, succession, regulator and public-service interfaces. Counsel validates legal sufficiency where needed.
3. **Map real work.** Select critical decisions and end-to-end workflows: routine purchase or repair, safety report, access issue, personnel matter, crisis notification, emergency expenditure, response-to-recovery handoff. Trace one recent actual instance of each where possible. Record initiator, information, approvals, wait time, decision, recipient, action, exception and outcome.
4. **Triangulate people and records.** Interview governing leaders, operators, frontline staff or volunteers, contractor representatives and dissenting/less-heard roles separately. Compare accounts with logs, meeting minutes, messages, work orders, financial controls, incident records and observation. Sampling and participation limits are stated.
5. **Trace promulgation.** For two consequential changes, identify approver, exact version, publication date, distribution, acknowledgement, comprehension, training, field use, obsolete-copy withdrawal and monitoring. Ask recipients to explain what they would do without prompting.
6. **Test normal and stress states.** Run a normal-operation trace, a degraded-resource scenario, a compound incident with competing demands, and an absent-leader/systems-down branch. At Level 1 this can be a documented tabletop hypothesis; Level 2 uses role interviews and evidence-backed walkthroughs; Level 3 uses realistic exercises and observed behavior with agreed safety controls.
7. **Detect patterns.** Compare decisions by type, role, time, location, workload and outcome. Look for recurring queues, unowned handoffs, repeated overrides, concentrated approval, unresolved exceptions, communication failures, silence after near misses, and good local innovations. Ask what else could explain the pattern; do not turn correlation into personal allegations.
8. **Validate and decide.** Provide factual findings to the Member for correction, preserve dissent, obtain specialist review of law/finance/engineering/clinical matters, and record accepted, disputed and unresolved evidence. Issue the appropriate level report only after human quality review.

Avoid collecting personal histories, private communications or identifiable allegations unless a lawful, authorized and necessary investigative scope exists. Assess roles and systems first. If a credible misconduct or threat indicator arises, use Salus's separately governed investigative, personnel, legal and safety process; do not publish speculation in a general organizational-health report.

## 5. Evidence and rating rules

For each domain, record **criterion, observation, source/date, sampling boundary, counterevidence, condition, cause hypothesis, consequence, existing strength, confidence and next validation step**. Distinguish:
- **Verified operating strength**: design, implementation and observed results align in representative conditions.
- **Designed but unproven**: document or testimony exists but operation has not been demonstrated.
- **Observed gap**: reliable evidence shows failure or absent control.
- **Conflicting evidence**: sources disagree; explain each and seek resolution.
- **Unknown**: insufficient authorized evidence. Do not score unknown as safe or failed.

Use an **ordinal 0–4 performance rating**, only where evidence supports it: 0 absent/failed; 1 ad hoc and person-dependent; 2 defined but inconsistently practiced; 3 operating reliably in routine work; 4 demonstrated under disruption and improved through review. Attach confidence **high / moderate / low** and a separate **consequence/urgency** judgment. “Unknown” is a valid unrated result. Do not average ratings into a single health number that masks a critical failure. A high consequence authority gap may outrank several low consequence process gaps.

For scenario testing, observe: correct authority invoked; decision time from trigger to authorized choice; percent of key recipients reached and able to explain direction; handoff completeness; objective and information quality; decision reversals attributable to missing information; essential-function interruption and restoration; resource sufficiency and fatigue; unapproved exceptions; corrective-action closure and retest. Set Member-specific targets before testing. Compare like periods and workloads, and state confounders.

## 6. Diagnostic questions for interviews and exercises

- What are the three most important decisions your role makes? Which require approval, and what happens when the approver is unavailable?
- Which work stops if one named person or vendor is absent for 72 hours? Who can lawfully and competently substitute?
- Describe a recent decision that changed after frontline information arrived. How did the information reach the decision maker?
- What direction was issued most recently? Show the current version, who received it, how understanding was checked, and how old instructions were retired.
- When did someone challenge a leader's assumption or report a near miss? What happened afterward?
- What issue repeatedly returns to the agenda or is handled through a workaround? What evidence distinguishes a capacity problem from a design or behavior problem?
- Which decisions are slow because authority is unclear, and which are appropriately slow because independent review protects the Member?
- What can be decided locally during a communications outage? What must wait? How are actions recorded and reconciled?
- When an incident shifts to recovery, who owns finance, claims, resident/employee communication, restoration and after-action closure?
- Which expected public or mutual-aid resource may be busy elsewhere? What is the fallback and how is it verified?

Ask for concrete examples, dates, records and outcomes. Offer confidential channels where appropriate, and avoid leading or punitive questions.

## 7. Recommendation design and causal justification

Do not recommend “better leadership” as a stand-alone remedy. For each priority issue, produce a **change case**:

1. **Observed condition and strength to preserve:** what happens today and what already works.
2. **Mechanism:** how ambiguity, queueing, suppressed information, missing skill, invalid authority or weak control causes a result.
3. **Options:** at least maintain/current control, a proportionate change, and where material a stronger alternative; compare cost, burden, legal fit and unintended effects.
4. **Selected intervention:** named owner, governing approval, scope, trigger, limits, resources, promulgation, training and independent safeguard.
5. **Expected benefit:** explicit normal-day and stress-day causal pathway; distinguish measured benefit from forecast.
6. **Proof plan:** baseline, target, owner, data source, test date, adverse effects, stop/revise trigger and residual risk.
7. **Follow-through:** SalOPS option/action, dependency, sequence, budget and governance decision; retest before calling it effective.

Examples to adapt, not automatic prescriptions:

| Observed mechanism | Proportionate option | Daily operation gain | Stress gain | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Approvals stall with one leader | Bounded delegation and trained alternates, legal review | Shorter routine queue without losing oversight | Essential decisions continue during absence | Sample approval times, exception and control results |
| Direction is issued but not understood | Versioned rule, audience map, receipt and teach-back | Fewer rework cycles | Consistent action during disruption | Recipient comprehension and observed exercise actions |
| Problems recur but near misses are not voiced | Protected reporting and nonpunitive learning review with accountability | Earlier correction and less hidden work | Weak signals surface before escalation | Reporting quality, closure and repeat rate; check for reporting suppression |
| Incident roles conflict with ordinary authority | Pre-agreed incident transition and command-transfer briefing | Cleaner role boundary | Faster coordinated response and recovery | Tabletop observations and handoff completeness |
| Board reacts to anecdotes | Decision brief with options, uncertainties, dissent and review date | More reproducible decisions | Better choices under uncertainty | Decision logs, reversals and subsequent outcomes |

Forecasts require testing; these mechanisms do not guarantee a numerical improvement.

## 8. Level-specific application and four-product flow

| Level | SalINTEL™ work | SalTHIRA™ use | SalOPS™ and SalCAPA™ output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1 — qualified initial picture** | Screen all 12 domains using available records, bounded interviews and open sources; identify strengths, gaps, contradictions, unknowns and exact Level 2 evidence requests | Apply the approved six steps at Level 1; treat leadership/command as cross-cutting drivers of scenario performance, capability targets, gaps and priorities; label assumptions | Offer proportionate Level 1 actions and options, including urgent interim safeguards and Level 2 readiness requirements. Integrated SalCAPA summary shows decision, owner, priority and confidence. |
| **Level 2 — focused validation** | Obtain Member records, trace decisions/processes, interview roles, validate authority and run selected walkthroughs; resolve material Level 1 unknowns | Re-estimate scenario and capability targets from validated Member evidence; compare alternatives and dependencies | Detailed implementation options, training, communication, cost/resource assumptions and measurement plan; identify questions needing Level 3 testing. |
| **Level 3 — deep demonstration** | Observe representative work, conduct appropriate exercises and longitudinal pattern analysis; independently validate consequential claims | Stress-test targets, interacting failures, recovery and resource competition; revise priorities and residual risks | Implement and retest approved changes, monitor outcomes, document residual risk and renewal triggers. |

All Members enter at Level 1. Each subsequent level depends on prior findings and authorization. Level 1 may still recommend or require an immediate SalOPS safeguard when delay would be material. Every report heading/front matter and executive summary identifies its level, what was actually tested, uncertainty and the next level's purpose. The diagnostic is a component within the existing four products, not a fifth product or duplicate report.

## 9. Report section and client education

Use a titled **Organizational Health, Leadership and Command** section in the relevant SalINTEL report. Begin with a plain-language explanation of why legitimate authority, rapid learning and reliable handoffs matter. Include:
1. scope and level;
2. strengths worth preserving;
3. organization/authority map;
4. domain table with rating, confidence, evidence and practical impact;
5. normal-day and stress-day narrative;
6. contradictions, legal applicability and missing evidence;
7. ranked changes with daily/stress benefit, burden, owner, test and residual risk;
8. SalTHIRA capability/scenario implications and SalOPS options;
9. questions and Member materials needed for the next level.

SalTHIRA incorporates the rated effects in its existing BLUF and six-step analysis. SalOPS converts approved options into actions and service opportunities without implying that purchasing services is the only remedy. SalCAPA gives the governing decision summary. Protect Member identities and Salus proprietary method detail in client outputs according to existing confidentiality rules.

## 10. Research basis, applicability and limits

This design synthesizes primary guidance, not a claim of certification or universal legal duty:

- [ISO 37000 governance overview](https://committee.iso.org/ISO_37000_Governance) and [current standard status](https://www.iso.org/standard/65036.html): purpose, oversight, accountability, leadership, decisions and risk governance. ISO states this is guidance, not new regulation.
- [ISO 22316 organizational resilience](https://www.iso.org/news/Ref2189.htm): shared values, changing context and empowered leadership.
- [ISO process approach](https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/archive/pdf/en/iso9001-2015-process-appr.pdf): process ownership, monitoring, improvement and risk-based proportionality. Check current edition and applicability at engagement.
- [GAO 2025 Green Book](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107721): objectives, authority, controls, information and monitoring. Binding scope is federal; use outside that scope as a reference only.
- [FEMA 2024 Continuity Guidance Circular](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_continuity-guidance-circular_082024.pdf): essential functions, succession, delegations, communications and continuity records.
- [FEMA NIMS management characteristics](https://emilms.fema.gov/_is0700b/groups/330.html) and [HSEEP](https://preptoolkit.fema.gov/web/hseep-resources/policy-and-guidance): incident coordination, exercise evaluation and improvement.
- [OPM Executive Core Qualifications, updated 2025](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/senior-executive-service/executive-core-qualifications/): leadership, judgment, agility and execution; leadership reference, not a mandate for private Members.
- [AHRQ safety-culture user guide](https://www.ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/wysiwyg/professionals/quality-patient-safety/patientsafetyculture/medical-office/userguide/medicalofficeusersguide.pdf): communication openness, process standardization and organizational learning. Healthcare instrument scores should not be transplanted into another sector.
- [NASA Columbia Accident Investigation Board report](https://sma.nasa.gov/SignificantIncidents/assets/columbia-accident-investigation-board-report-volume-1.pdf): organizational silence, informal command and overreliance on past success as failure patterns. This is an instructive case, not proof about any Member.
- [CDC Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication](https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual/index.html): timely, accurate, credible, empathetic and actionable crisis messages.

Research boundary: sources above were reviewed as of 28 September 2026. This is a cross-sector method. Before making a Member finding, the analyst must identify jurisdiction, legal form, sector rules, adopted standards, contracts, insurance terms, regulator guidance, current versions, and contrary evidence. Quantitative outcome claims require Member baseline and retest. No Member has been assessed by this proposal.

## 11. Ongoing method conformance test

A Salus analyst applies this method only when they can: (1) map legal and real authority; (2) show a verified strength and a justified gap in each material domain; (3) reproduce a decision and promulgation trace; (4) distinguish routine and stress performance; (5) rate evidence without treating unknown as failure; (6) connect each recommendation to daily and stress benefit and a measurable retest; (7) carry findings through the existing Level 1–3 and four-product structure; and (8) protect Member and personnel data. SAL-D051 records Marc's approval of this member-neutral method. Each Member application, report-template revision, operational change or software implementation remains subject to its separate scope, evidence, conformance and human release gates.
