# Salus Planning Standard

**Requirement:** SALUS-PLANNING-001. **Decision:** SAL-D154. **Owner direction:** 23 November 2026. **Scope:** all Salus planning work. **Handling:** Salus Internal. The owner authorized incorporation; this exact repository implementation remains a candidate until governed promotion.

## Purpose and common process

A Salus plan must help people make sound decisions and take clear steps. Keep complex analysis in linked assessments or supporting records. Make the plan, its concepts, role cards and message forms usable under the conditions in which people need them. Apply the requirements below to every plan and material revision. Add relevant topic-specific requirements; record a reason and evidence when an item does not apply. A reason cannot waive a binding duty, material evidence gap or protected approval.

Use one linked planning record through six stages: scope the task; establish evidence and duties; compare options; write feasible steps; review and test; approve, issue and maintain. Record entry inputs, output, owner, acceptance proof and remaining conditions for each stage. Do not confuse these planning stages with SalTHIRA's six steps or commercial assessment levels. Preserve the approved six-step method, five mission areas, relevant capability applicability screen and same-level SalINTEL → SalTHIRA → SalOPS → integrated SalCAPA chain. SalINTEL owns sources and uncertainty; SalTHIRA owns risk, capability targets and priorities; SalOPS owns feasible options and separately authorized performance; SalCAPA owns integrated decisions and closure records.

## P01 Set the scope and decision

State the purpose, intended decisions, plan owner, users, affected people, covered functions and sites, planning horizon, deadline, assumptions, exclusions and decision authority. Establish the exact assessment or other evidence baseline and existing plan identity. Identify the actual start, milestones and capacity without silently resetting an assessment's existing 24-hour clock. Use proportionate depth without omitting required checks.

## P02 Review and qualify evidence

Apply existing research-entry, all-location consideration, source qualification, capture, rights and privacy rules. Review relevant originals, current and earlier versions, linked material and contrary evidence. Record exact sources, dates/versions, portions reviewed, where-used links, limitations and decision effects. Distinguish discovery, metadata screening, reading, qualification and approved use. Existing evidence is reused only within its verified scope and currency. Unavailable material remains a visible gap with an owner and next proof; a count, bibliography or template is not proof of review or compliance.

## P03 Map applicable duties

For each material legal, contractual, policy or adopted-standard requirement, record the source and current version, jurisdiction and entity/activity applicability, trigger, required action, responsible authority, recipient, due time, required content, retention and verification evidence. Distinguish binding law, contract, internal policy, guidance and voluntary performance targets. Identify decision-changing professional questions and qualified review needs. Do not infer a local duty, exemption, legal privilege, insurance coverage or certification from generic guidance, an organizational label or a public standards summary. Resolve material applicability gaps before the corresponding approval, use or compliance claim.

## P04 Link evidence to action

Give each material action a stable identity and link it to the accepted finding, scenario/target/gap or explicit planning requirement. Keep the plain-language rationale and executable checklist consistent. Distinguish facts, reported practice, assumptions, unknowns and judgment. Written instructions do not prove operational performance; missing evidence does not prove a defect. Use the existing governed action/decision record and update the existing plan identity rather than creating duplicate reports or registers.

## P05 Compare choices and consequences

Challenge assumptions and weaknesses; compare practical alternatives and no action; reconcile conflicting evidence; examine unintended effects. Preserve strengths worth retaining. Explain why a choice fits the decision, affected people and actual setting. Compare time, resource competition, authority, accessibility, privacy, safety, reversibility, funding, life-cycle cost and residual exposure. State what new fact would reverse or change the recommendation. Do not quantify benefit or superiority without defensible evidence and a named comparison boundary.

## P06 Make the steps feasible

State start/escalation triggers, ordered tasks, primary and alternate roles, decision and stop authority, partner interfaces, accepted handoffs, milestones, resources, cost basis and funding/payment responsibility, dependencies, constraints, safe fallback, completion proof and retest triggers. Confirm actual partner capacity and authority rather than assuming acceptance from a notification or agreement. Check concurrent demands, key-person loss and common-mode failures. A proposed role, tool, budget or service is not an approved or demonstrated capability. Separately authorize implementation, purchases and operations where required.

For incident-related plans, distinguish immediate danger, rapidly changing events, slower developing concerns and sustained response/recovery. Match procedure, approval/delegation, verification, update pace, staffing and fallback to time-to-harm, rate of change and applicable deadlines. Define transitions and parallel urgent/extended tracks. A slower event can still have an immediate required action; an initial label must not freeze the response pace.

## P07 Use third-grade plain language

The required writing target for plans, their concepts, role cards and message templates is a third-grade reading level. Use common words, short sentences, direct verbs and one task per step. Explain the concept itself simply; shortening sentences alone is insufficient. Put the user's next action first. Keep the detail and complex analysis in supporting assessment or analysis records, with clear links from the plan.

Screen actionable sections and templates individually; an overall average must not hide a difficult critical instruction. State the method, actual text boundary, result, edits and limits of any reading score. Preserve exact names, locations, necessary terms, deadlines, legal/safety meaning and accessible alternatives. Explain necessary terms simply; keep an unalterable quotation or mandated notice in its proper supporting record with a plain explanation, without omitting an applicable duty. Correct unclear or overly difficult passages and retest. Readability formulas support editing but do not establish comprehension, third-grade suitability or safe action by themselves. Verify intended-user understanding and task performance under the existing applicable assurance requirements; do not invent human feedback or impose a duplicate unfamiliar-reader approval gate.

## P08 Design for affected people and failure

Map the actual users and affected groups, their information/action needs, language and disability/access needs, available channels and privacy limits. Include visitors and people without assumed devices or connectivity. Distinguish sending, receipt, understanding, action and accepted transfer of responsibility. Provide trusted message sources, primary and alternate communication paths, offline aids where needed, secure account access, and safe operation during loss of a key person, tool, supplier, power or network. Keep credentials in the approved secret store and sensitive details in restricted records. Confirm backup paths through evidence; two channels sharing one failed dependency are not independent resilience.

## P09 Review coherent revisions

Use the assessment's recorded process lessons and one reconciled evidence baseline. Resolve related changes in coherent batches before rebuilding the plan; preserve prior versions, material changes and their effects. Perform at least eight distinct substantive re-passes followed by a separate terminal editorial pass under the existing task-review requirement. Record the actual question, evidence, conclusion, correction or reason to retain the result for each pass. Recheck affected conclusions after material changes. The assistant performs routine conformance, corrections and proof; substantive human judgments and approvals keep their actual scope. Never count software as personnel or repeat one check under several labels.

## P10 Verify exact documents

Apply current Salus branding, prescribed fonts/size floors, heading spacing, confidentiality, version/date, applicable cover/blank-page/footer rules, accessible structure and referenced Federal/OPM guidance. Do not describe the Salus font pair as a universal OPM mandate. Check every exact offered format, page, table, link, role route and print/save layout. Render and inspect the exact final files after material edits; use native Word when available and record unavailable physical-print, assistive-user or other paths. Retain hashes, page counts, renderer, scope, defects, fixes and final disposition. Prior template approval and automated checks alone do not validate populated pages.

## P11 Set measures and test outcomes

Before a test, record its purpose, audience, scenario, conditions, target and acceptance measure, safe scope, owner, observer and evidence method. Test proportionate findability, understanding, correct next action, timing, reach, backup operation, partner handoffs, correction and sustained use. Mark test traffic and isolate it from live channels. Record actual outcomes, constraints, failures, residual gaps, corrective actions and retest. Do not claim readiness from a written plan, attendance, purchase, sent message or closed ticket alone.

Excellence requires a bounded benchmark: name the reference guidance and lawfully available comparable plans, common tasks/conditions, measures, sample and limitations before claiming a better result. Seek improvements in useful outcomes rather than document length or rhetoric. No claim to outperform every other plan follows from authoring or self-review. A target is not a measured result; passing a reading score is not proof that people can act.

## P12 Approve and maintain controlled plans

Keep draft, checked, approved, issued, authorized, performed, verified and sustained/closed states distinct. Record the competent authority, exact version, scope, conditions, effective period and distribution permission for each decision. Preserve prior decisions within their original scope; do not seek the same completed approval again. Issue through the existing governed identity, retain predecessors and update existing records rather than producing duplicates.

Set the owner, review schedule and triggers: incidents, exercises, changed people/sites/tools/contracts/law, new evidence, failed performance or changed risk. Update material dependencies and related outputs together. Maintain change history, open questions, residual exposure, action owners, corrective proof, retest and lessons. Transfer only qualified member-neutral learning to enterprise records; keep Protected Member material in its authorized boundary. Policy adoption does not prove artifact migration, operational implementation or deployed enforcement.

## Crisis communications module and evidence record

Apply `SALUS_CRISIS_COMMUNICATIONS_PLANNING_STANDARD.md` for crisis communications plans. Other planning domains add their own applicable modules without substituting for P01–P12. For each requirement, retain its plan/record locator, evidence identity, acceptance check, result, defects/corrections and remaining gap in the existing task/plan assurance record. A not-applicable disposition needs a reason. Missing evidence or unfinished implementation must not become a checked completion claim.

## Continuous authorized work on every Salus task

SAL-D153 / SALUS-CONTINUOUS-WORK-001 applies to all Salus work, not only plans. Continue the authorized task through completion, including ordinary research, drafting, checks, correction, preparation and handoff, without routine confirmation stops or asking permission already given. If one step is blocked, continue independent authorized work. Give concise progress updates and preserve task continuity across interruptions.

Pause only when the next dependent action requires an unavoidable user decision, protected authorization, missing access or security boundary. Identify the actual blocker, the governing reason and the exact needed decision/action; prepare all unaffected and reviewable work first. A pending or unanswered question is not approval. Honor user stops and scope changes. Do not cross a hold, invent evidence, loosen qualification, restart a paused engagement, activate a reader/monitor, spend, send, merge or deploy merely to avoid stopping. Ordinary task completion is a truthful handoff, not an obligation to invent further work. Do not promise unattended execution beyond the environment's actual capability. This rule preserves existing exact-revision and consequential-action controls.

## Research and authority basis

The owner's planning, simple-language and continuity directions authorize these enterprise requirements. Selected CDC CERC material supports audience/action-oriented crisis communication; the CDC Clear Communication Index describes why reading formulas alone cannot prove clarity; OPM describes reader-centered plain language; NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 RS.CO/RC.CO distinguishes reporting, coordination, public updates and recovery. These are bounded method references, not proof of Member applicability, legal compliance, rights clearance or performance. Exact task-specific source scopes and unresolved access/corpus gaps remain in restricted source records.
