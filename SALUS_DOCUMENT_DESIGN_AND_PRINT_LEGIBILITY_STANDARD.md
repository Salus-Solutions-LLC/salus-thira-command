# Salus Document Design and Print Legibility Standard

**Authority:** SAL-D128, Marc's 3 November 2026 direction. **Handling:** Salus Internal. **State:** governed documentation candidate pending repository checks, exact-head approval and merge.

## Purpose and applicability

This standard controls typography, cover composition and print legibility for every new or materially revised Salus document, including reports, white papers, assessments, proposals, agreements, questionnaires, plans, methods, presentations, forms, templates and internal controlled records. It supplements the approved Salus templates and applicable federal/OPM guidance. When a format cannot technically meet the standard, record the exception, reason, accessible alternative, owner and correction path before release; do not silently reduce text below the minimum.

## Cover-page composition

For every document with a cover logo and title:

- preserve at least **36 points (0.5 inch)** of clear vertical white space between the bottom of the approved Salus logo/name lockup and the first title line;
- increase that separation when needed to keep the cover open, balanced and visually calm;
- keep the logo/name lockup, title, subtitle, confidentiality marking, date and version from crowding or visually colliding; and
- render and inspect the cover at full-page size and at 100-percent print scale.

The 36-point separation is a minimum, not a target that prevents additional white space. Cover-bearing documents that cannot provide it require a governed layout correction before release.

## Typography minimums

- Use **12-point Times New Roman** for ordinary body paragraphs. Body text may be larger when accessibility, audience or medium warrants it, but never smaller.
- Use **at least 10-point text in every table**, including table headings, cells, notes and sources. Increase row height, column width, page count or table continuation instead of shrinking text.
- Never use text smaller than **10 points anywhere in a Salus document**. This absolute floor includes headers, footers, page numbers, footnotes, endnotes, captions, figure and chart labels, legends, source notes, citations, references, disclaimers, confidentiality markings, callouts, appendices, form instructions and metadata intended for the reader.
- Preserve the approved Salus typography: Arial for titles, headings, page headers and footers; Times New Roman for body text. Footer text, including the normal-weight words “Salus Solutions,” telephone number, contact email and page numbering, must be Arial, at least 10 points, kept on the approved shared baseline and not wrapped mid-name.
- Do not evade these requirements through font scaling, condensed character spacing, image-based text, reduced print scaling or automatic “fit text” behavior.

## Layout adjustments

Accommodate the larger type by increasing page count, table row height, cell padding, column width, paragraph spacing, header/footer distance, or section layout as necessary. Repeat table header rows across pages, keep words intact rather than wrapping them mid-word, and split or reorient complex tables when needed. Adjust header and footer spacing so their text remains clear of body content and printer nonprintable areas. Do not reduce margins or crowd page furniture merely to preserve a prior page count.

## Release validation

Before release, inspect the exact DOCX, PDF and print/save version offered to the reader. At minimum:

1. verify body text is 12 points or larger;
2. verify every table element is 10 points or larger;
3. scan the complete document for any text below 10 points, including headers, footers, notes and drawing objects;
4. verify the cover's logo-to-title separation is at least 36 points and visually balanced;
5. print or render at 100-percent scale and inspect every page for readability, clipping, overflow, table continuity, mid-word wrapping and header/footer collisions; and
6. record the exact file hash, renderer or printer path, page count, defects, corrections, rerender and reviewer.

Automated font inspection is a required screen but does not replace full-page visual and print review. A compliant source template does not prove that a populated document remains compliant.

## Existing documents and templates

Apply this standard prospectively to every new or materially revised document. Do not silently replace an issued predecessor. Existing templates, working reports or approved layout candidates that contain smaller text or insufficient cover spacing require controlled successor versions, exact-file validation, artifact registration and the applicable human release decision before future use. Prior approval of a different layout does not waive this standard for a new or materially revised document.
