# Salus supply-chain resilience assessment standard

**Authority:** SAL-D057, 28 September 2026. **Scope:** Member-neutral SalCAPA™, SalINTEL™, SalTHIRA™ and SalOPS™ method at sequential Levels 1–3, with a low-burden Protection Advisor introduction. It applies to products, services, public missions, estates, farms and ranches. The standard covers both **inbound survival inputs** and **outbound commitments or products**. It is a method, not a finding that any Protected Member or supplier is deficient.

## 1. What the assessment must answer

For each essential function and each product/service the Member produces, moves or delivers: What inputs, people, utilities, information, rights, logistics and buyers does it depend on? How long can it operate at a stated minimum level if each is interrupted? How long will restoration or a qualified substitute actually take? Who decides how scarce resources are allocated? What daily controls prevent avoidable disruption, and what changes under stress? Use the Member's actual operating model, output obligations and welfare/safety thresholds, rather than a generic supplier questionnaire.

The method examines **plan → source → make/operate → store → move → deliver → return/recover** and the enabling money, authority, workforce, cyber/data, energy, water and public-service networks. For livestock/exotics, output may be animals or agricultural production while indispensable inputs include water, feed/forage, veterinary care, fencing, transport, refrigeration and lawful movement. A nonproducer can still have a critical supply chain for its essential services. Distinguish an item that can be delayed from a life-safety, animal-welfare or statutory minimum.

## 2. Scientific and professional basis, with limits

| Concept | Salus use | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Business process and impact analysis | Map the function, minimum acceptable output, maximum tolerable interruption, restoration order and resource thresholds. | A plan or interview alone does not demonstrate performance. |
| Directed supply network and criticality | Represent supplier, site, transport, utility, data, output and customer dependencies; look for single points, shared tiers, geographic clustering and cut sets. | Unknown second/third tiers and informal dependencies can make the graph falsely complete. |
| Time to survive (TTS) and time to recover (TTR) | Compare the time an output can be maintained after a specific input fails with the time to restore or qualify replacement capacity. Escalate when TTR exceeds TTS. | Both are scenario-, demand- and minimum-service-specific estimates; a single days-of-stock figure is not TTS. |
| Inventory and flow dynamics | Test demand variability, reorder/transport lead time, spoilage, storage, capacity and information distortion, including order amplification during panic buying. | A numerical safety stock formula needs credible demand and lead-time data; correlated regional shortages defeat naive independence assumptions. |
| Failure modes and scenario stress | Test loss of supplier, route, utility, workforce, payment system, data, permit, buyer or several together; include recovery and adverse effects of substitutions. | Do not fabricate probabilities from sparse incident records. |
| Resource allocation under constraints | Compare no-action, stock, alternate supplier/route, process change, demand reduction, mutual aid and capital options against safety, feasibility, cost, equity and residual risk. | Unapproved prices, partner availability and legal rights are not commitments. |
| Continuous sensing and learning | Monitor leading and lagging indicators, test triggers, capture incidents/near misses, challenge assumptions and revise approved plans. | Public industry data are context, not evidence of a named vendor's inventory or performance. |

These concepts draw on [FEMA continuity/BIA guidance](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_continuity-guidance-circular_082024.pdf), [CISA's infrastructure-dependency primer](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/critical-infrastructure-security-and-resilience/resilience-services/infrastructure-dependency-primer/learn), [MIT TTS/TTR research](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/bd06d08c-ebeb-4fa8-bbea-db56193b156d), the original [bullwhip-effect study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.43.4.546), and [NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final) for ICT supplier criticality and provenance. NIST's federal ICT controls are a transferable lens, not automatically binding on every Member. The analyst records which source applies, its edition, date and uncertainty.

## 3. Minimum data model and collection order

Begin with **outputs and minimum operation**, not a request for all invoices. For each critical output/function, record `output_id, owner, customer/beneficiary, required volume/quality, ordinary demand, surge/seasonal demand, safety/welfare floor, interruption tolerance, regulatory/contract obligation, recovery priority`. Then map only the inputs whose loss could change that decision: `input_id, specification, quantity/rate, inventory usable, shelf life, reorder point, ordinary/emergency lead time, source and tier, site/geography, transport route/mode, storage, quality/approval gate, substitute and qualification time, contract/payment constraint, data/IT dependency, responsible role`. Preserve units and dates.

Use a staged request: (1) Member interview and existing essential-function/finance/incident records; (2) purchase, inventory, usage, contract, shipment, supplier and maintenance data for **named critical items**; (3) supplier/utility/responder confirmation and field observation only for decision-changing gaps. Capture outbound orders, capacity, delivery commitments, transport, customer concentration, returns, recalls and cash conversion where applicable. Request restricted prices, customer lists, veterinary/medical details and security-sensitive maps only through the engagement boundary. Do not ask anonymous Protection Advisor Part 1 for a full supplier list.

For each material chain, record a stable `supply_chain_id` and link function/output → input → supplier/tier/site → route/lifeline → evidence/assumption → scenario/capability target/gap → option/decision/proof. Separate **observed**, **supplier reported**, **industry inferred**, **modelled**, **assumed**, **contradicted** and **unknown** values. Never turn a missing document into a supplier-failure finding.

## 4. Daily and stressed-operation tests

**Daily control:** reconcile forecast versus actual consumption and output; inventory accuracy; specification/quality; supplier on-time-in-full performance; lead time variance; rejected goods; maintenance spares; expiring stock; purchase authority; payment/cash; demand/customer changes; and incident/near-miss correction. Evaluate whether key people know the process and whether a backup can perform it.

**Stress test:** for a defined outage horizon, remove one or more suppliers, routes, power/water/communications, staff, finance/payment or buyers. Recalculate usable stock, minimum output, substitute approval time, delivery capability and TTS/TTR. Test at least one regional simultaneous-demand case, one common-mode dependency and one erroneous-information case. Include degraded operation, safe shutdown, animal/welfare or public-health obligations, ramp-up and return to normal. Ask which substitute introduces a different hazard, contractual breach, insurance issue or cost spike. Compare an optimistic assumption with a conservative one; label untested recovery times.

Where measured data allow, `usable days of supply = usable quantity ÷ scenario daily use`, with perishability, inaccessible stock and surge adjustments stated. `TTS` is the first time required output drops below the defined minimum after available buffers, alternate capacity and allocation are applied. `TTR` is the elapsed time until that output is again sustainable, not merely the supplier's announced restart. The `TTR − TTS` gap is a **scenario decision aid**, not a universal risk score. Use a quantitative model only when input dates, units, distributions, correlations and validation warrant it; otherwise use bounded ranges and qualitative priorities.

## 5. Evidence acquisition and update system

| Source tier | Examples and use | Refresh and verification rule |
|---|---|---|
| Member operational truth | Inventory/ERP, purchase orders, bills of materials or service resource lists, usage, maintenance, route, supplier contracts, output/quality, financial and incident records | Critical quantities at the Member's operating cadence; reconcile system values with a sample of physical/financial records. Owner signs off on tolerances. |
| Direct counterpart | Supplier capacity/stock and alternate sites, carrier lanes, utility outage/recovery, emergency-service availability, customer demand | Confirm on onboarding, material change and test; a generic business-continuity certificate is not proof of current deliverable capacity. |
| Government and regulated sources | [CISA dependencies](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/critical-infrastructure-security-and-resilience/resilience-services/infrastructure-dependency-primer/learn), [USDA AMS Market News](https://www.ams.usda.gov/market-news), [EIA data](https://www.eia.gov/opendata/), [USITC DataWeb](https://www.usitc.gov/applications/dataweb/about), [BEA input-output](https://bea.gov/data/industries/input-output-accounts-data), [Census trade data](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/international-trade.html), weather/drought/road and applicable regulator notices | Record each dataset's publication lag, unit, sector/geography, revision and API/license. These show macro exposure, not supplier-specific stock or delivery. Monitor update cadence relevant to the product and jurisdiction. |
| Research and commercial intelligence | Peer-reviewed models, industry associations, licensed shipment/credit/geospatial data, insurer and broker analysis | Check method, sample, rights, bias, date and conflict of interest; corroborate consequential claims. Do not expose another Member's identifiable data. |

Maintain a **source registry** with URL/record pointer, publisher, scope, acquisition/publication/last-check dates, revision, access rights, reliability, applicability, contradiction and next check. Configure event-driven review for warning of supplier closure, outage, labor action, recall, animal disease, route/flood/fire closure, tariff/sanction change, demand spike, cash constraint or loss of a sole qualified substitute. An analyst validates an alert before it changes a finding or operational task. Default cadence is set per item and season: daily/weekly for life-safety or rapidly consumed inputs, monthly for key supplier/price/lead-time trends, and quarterly/annual for slower network or policy structure; the Member approves actual thresholds and staffing. This is a monitoring design, not an always-on service entitlement.

## 6. Assessment and prioritization

For each chain, show **output criticality, safety/welfare consequence, interruption threshold, observed TTS/TTR range, concentration and shared geography, supplier/route alternatives, quality/legal qualification time, financial feasibility, evidence confidence and competing regional demand**. A single color or composite score must not hide a catastrophic safety failure or a missing critical input. Rank **attention and decision urgency**, with rationale and sensitivity to the main assumption. Distinguish preventable daily inefficiency from disruption survivability and recovery capacity. Check whether a proposed buffer crowds out another critical input or expires before use.

Compare options against no-action: demand shaping and transparent ordering; inventory or distributed stock; dual/alternate sourcing with independently tested capacity; contract and supplier-development changes; route/utility diversification; emergency purchasing/cash authority; local production or repair; information-sharing and cyber controls; safe shutdown and welfare plans; insurance/claims and financial recovery. State causal mechanism, ordinary-day benefit, stressed-day benefit, owner, rights, resources, lead time, competing resources, proof and residual risk. All recommendations pair a narrative with an action checklist under SAL-D055. A proposed Salus service is optional, evidence-linked and separately scoped.

## 7. Levels, products and report requirements

| Product | Level 1 Initial Pass | Level 2 Focused Assessment | Level 3 Deep Dive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protection Advisor | One or two educational questions on essential inputs/output and known sole source; show a preliminary hypothesis and voluntary Level 1 step. | No direct entry; authenticated Part 2 may identify evidence availability without a full vendor upload. | No direct entry. |
| SalINTEL™ | Explain the Member's value chain and survival inputs; inventory source/incident evidence, a minimum dependency map, qualitative TTS/TTR, confidence, contradictions and tailored missing-data table. | Validate selected suppliers, inventory, lead times, geography, contracts, output obligations and actual recovery with counterpart/field evidence. | Trace approved multi-tier and global dependencies, scenario data, correlated exposure and sensitivity. |
| SalTHIRA™ | Carry the chain into all six steps and applicable five mission areas/32 capabilities; set bounded output and endurance targets, compare present capacity/gaps and rank no-action/treatments. | Reassess selected targets and gaps with measured TTS/TTR, tested alternates and actual demand. | Model approved compound networks, uncertainty and adaptive recovery/resource allocation. |
| SalOPS™ | Specific, feasible supplier, stock, route, exercise, monitoring and safe-operation options; no automatic execution or purchase. | Scope and test approved qualification, contracts, logistics and trigger playbooks. | Execute separately authorized network redesign, exercises and continuous assurance. |
| SalCAPA™ | One same-level decision summary linking essential output, critical input, evidence, assumption, target/gap, option, owner and proof. | Record validated changes, funding and residual exposure. | Integrate compound-system decisions, monitoring and governed lessons. |

Each stand-alone report marks represented level and completion status on its confidential cover and executive summary, educates the Member on the method's meaning/limits, and identifies next-level work only when it could change a decision. The SalINTEL™ report includes a titled **Supply chain and essential-input resilience** section with an input/output map, critical-chain table, narrative plus checklist priorities, source dates and missing-proof request. SalTHIRA™ includes same-ID target, gap and sensitivity decisions; SalOPS™ contains separately authorized options; SalCAPA™ reconciles exact approved versions. Preserve the blank second page, `page X of Y`, Arial headings/Times New Roman body, Member isolation, all-page render/accessibility review and one governed report identity under the Charter.

## 8. Specialist and governance gates

Determine actual law/contract/permit/insurance applicability for food, livestock movement, veterinary products, chemicals, fuel, transport, labor, procurement, sanctions, privacy and cyber before stating a duty or compliance failure. Use qualified counsel, veterinarian, engineer, CPA, broker, logistics specialist or utility/operator where the finding depends on their competence. Background inquiries into suppliers or people require lawful purpose, proportional collection, contrary evidence and human review. The Member owns acceptance of scarce-resource allocation and any operational, financial or contractual action. Salus records assumptions and recommends options; it does not imply a supplier guarantee, insurance coverage or permanent monitoring unless authorized.

Report corrections, source revisions, incidents and exercise results through SAL-D025 governed learning and SAL-D027 report impact. Member-specific facts stay in the engagement boundary. Generalize only reviewed methods/lessons for enterprise reuse. No document or dashboard counter proves that the whole supply chain is known.
