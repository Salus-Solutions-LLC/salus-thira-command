# Salus expanded lens discovery and past-chat reconciliation method

**Status:** Approved member-neutral method under SAL-D054, 28 September 2026. **Owner:** Salus Solutions. **Handling:** Salus Internal Confidential. **Applies to:** Protection Advisor, SalINTEL™, SalTHIRA™, SalOPS™ and SalCAPA™ at Levels 1–3. Use with SAL-D025, SAL-D050–D053, the Program Charter, required-information crosswalk and current approved product standards. This method adds conditional questions and a discovery loop; it does not create a fifth product, alter SalTHIRA's six steps or 32 core capabilities, revise a Word template, deploy software, or issue a Protected Member finding.

## 1. Research boundary and governing distinction

This review compared the existing 18-perspective SAL-D050 register, the 12-domain SAL-D051 organizational-health method, SAL-D053 information crosswalk, and approved product standards with primary public sources and accessible past chats on 28 September 2026. The 18 perspectives remain the parent taxonomy. Rows below are **specific sub-lenses or stress tests** that were implicit, under-specified or absent; they are not 18 additional mandatory disciplines. The analyst screens them for applicability to the Protected Member's objectives, place, activities and authority. A concern becomes a Member finding only after evidence, contradiction review, analyst qualification and the applicable human release gate.

The app exposed the 50 most recent non-pinned chats and three pinned chats; 49 of the 50 non-pinned were in this Salus project. Its limit is 50 and no older-page cursor was offered. A separate archived-chat listing exposed 43 records, none tagged to this project. This review inspected the turns of all 48 app-visible prior non-pinned Salus-project chats through bounded message previews (each exposed chat page reported no more turns); some long messages and attachments were truncated or unavailable (the 49th project chat is this active task). It also sampled ten turns of a pinned Salus recovery task, whose older turns remain available but were not all examined. The 43 archived-chat titles were checked for project association, not read in full. This is a **bounded accessible-chat review**, not an assertion that every prior Salus chat, attachment, OneDrive or SharePoint record was examined. The current chat's user decisions were also compared against governed records. Chat titles, assistant statements and attachments are leads, never proof of deployed status, factual allegations or approved authority.

## 2. Distinctive sub-lenses to screen

| Sub-lens and SAL-D050 parent | Decision-changing test; evidence and competent counterpart | Level 2/3 trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Owner, governing body and operator (risk/governance) | Who owns the objective, property, activity and decision? Reconcile governing instruments, covenants, delegated powers, insurance named entities, maintenance and emergency spending authority with actual practice. | Conflicting authority, unbudgeted life-safety duty or unowned essential function. |
| Police, dispatch and justice system (emergency management/behavioral threat) | Confirm jurisdiction, 911 routing, dispatch handoff, access instructions, response-time distribution, concurrent-call capacity, evidentiary preservation and victim support with the responsible agencies; distinguish reported delay from measured performance. | Remote access, high-consequence response delay, credible intentional threat or disputed jurisdiction. |
| Fire service and community risk reduction (emergency management/engineering) | Compare response area, water supply, apparatus access, wildfire/structure exposure, prevention inspection, mutual aid and firefighter safety; validate routes and water availability with responders. | Access bottleneck, WUI exposure, inadequate water or reliance on unconfirmed volunteers/mutual aid. |
| EMS, trauma and medical systems (public health/emergency management) | Map dispatch, ambulance/air medical availability, transfer destinations, time-sensitive care, medical surge, alternate route and public-health interface. Use aggregate assistance planning; restrict health details. | Long transport, simultaneous incidents, seasonal surge, access loss or specialized care need. |
| HAZMAT and process safety (environment/compliance) | Inventory activities, transport corridors and chemicals at a proportionate level; check EPCRA/OSHA/transport applicability, containment, notification, responder awareness and exposure pathways. | Threshold chemical, unknown inventory, incompatible storage, contamination pathway or specialist response gap. |
| Planning, zoning, code and floodplain authority (regulatory/engineering) | Verify the actual jurisdiction, parcel, use, floodplain map and adopted ordinance/code edition; compare permitted versus actual use, development/repair permits, drainage, access and elevation evidence. | New use or construction, post-disaster repair, mapped/observed flood conflict or missing permits. |
| Meteorology, hydrology and geospatial uncertainty (environment/engineering) | Combine official forecasts, recorded events, terrain, drainage, road low points and map limitations; distinguish mapped boundary from observed inundation and short-fuse warning uncertainty. | Compound rain/flash-flood, wildfire-weather transition, map dispute or evacuation-time sensitivity. |
| Transportation, evacuation and accessibility (continuity/public health) | Test route capacity, gates, bridges, private roads, responder entry, two-way conflict, vehicle mix, accessible transport, reunification, supply delivery and route loss. | Single route, constrained bridge, flood/wildfire overlap, visitor surge or mobility-dependent population. |
| Aviation, recreation and animal interface (environment/animal health) | Where present, screen private aircraft/landing zones, equestrian activity, events, animal movement, visitor injury, veterinary care and rescue interfaces; use actual operations, not generic hazards. | Aircraft operations, large animal numbers, public events or rescue resource specialization. |
| Agriculture, livestock, exotics and One Health (animal health/public health) | Identify species and activity, feed/water dependence, worker and visitor contact, veterinary capacity, biosecurity, animal disease reporting, carcass/disposal and evacuation/shelter feasibility. | Disease suspicion, mixed species, transport restrictions, severe weather or contaminated water. |
| Power, fuel, water, communications and cyber/OT coupling (energy/cyber) | Draw a dependency chain for critical functions: grid, generator, fuel, well pump, treatment, radio/cellular/Internet, access controls, cloud and staff. Test common-mode and restart sequence, not just individual backups. | Multiple lifelines share fuel, route, supplier, control system or storm footprint. |
| Information integrity and AI/automation (cyber/human factors) | Check source authentication, rumor/misinformation, data lineage, automated recommendations, model drift, manual fallback and human override; test whether warnings are comprehended and acted on. | Automated consequential decision, low-trust audience, degraded communications or manipulated information. |
| Occupational safety, fatigue and spontaneous volunteers (worker safety/emergency management) | Identify who may work, train, supervise and stop unsafe tasks; examine PPE, fatigue, chainsaws/heavy equipment, traffic control and insurance/indemnity of volunteers. | Post-disaster cleanup, untrained volunteers, hazardous equipment or prolonged shifts. |
| Recovery of housing, economy, culture and ecology (continuity/sociology) | Beyond asset repair, ask how displacement, employment, local business, schools, health, cultural sites and environmental function recover; identify who bears delays and funding gaps. | Long restoration, uninsured loss, resident/workforce displacement or ecological damage. |
| Public-service competition and civic economy (public safety/finance) | Check whether regional events draw from the same responders, hospitals, fuel, contractors, shelters, grants or political attention; document actual agreements and prioritization authority. | Concurrent disasters, regional shortage, rural/seasonal surge or reliance on informal promises. |
| Supply-chain geography and regulation (procurement/finance) | Trace critical single-source parts, fuel, medicines, feed, contractors and cloud services across jurisdictions; screen export, sanctions, tariffs and regulatory changes only where relevant and current. | Long lead time, foreign concentration, unstable rule or inability to substitute. |
| Insurance, reconstruction and capital sequencing (insurance/finance/engineering) | Reconcile coverage terms, claims notice, exclusions, deductibles, replacement cost, code upgrades, liquidity and permit lead time; model what can actually be restored and when. | Coverage uncertainty, major uninsured exposure, covenant pressure or phased repair. |
| Community trust, political context and distribution (sociology/governance) | Identify formal and informal authority, competing narratives, historically underserved groups, perceived fairness, conflict and consent without labeling political views as threats. Verify representation and lawful stakeholder participation. | Untrusted warning source, contested land use, unequal burden or decisions affecting non-members. |
| Adversarial and second-order effects (independent review/risk) | Ask what a treatment makes worse: displaced risk, privacy loss, dependency on one provider, counterparty reaction, volunteer injury, unsafe evacuation, ecological impact or financial crowd-out. | High-cost, irreversible or coercive measure; critical assumption drives ranking. |

These screens complement existing SAL-D050 anti-bribery, insider/vendor, financial, insurance, infrastructure, regulatory, continuity, accessibility and independent-challenge requirements. A broad parent lens being present does not establish that the operational question was answered.

## 3. Discovery method for knowns and surprises

For each material scenario, separate: **verified observation**, **sourced report not independently confirmed**, **inference**, **assumption**, **known evidence gap**, **disputed account**, and **unmodeled possibility**. Record source/time/geography, confidence, owner and a falsifying observation. Do not score an unknown as zero risk or as a proven high risk. “Unknown unknowns” cannot be itemized in advance; build ways to notice them:

1. Interview different roles and affected people; compare official design with routine and stressed practice. Preserve protected-class, health and person-level privacy.
2. Reconstruct actual incidents, near misses, workarounds and external after-action lessons. Search for surprises outside the current scenario list, then check local applicability.
3. Draw functional dependency maps and failure paths across mission areas; test loss of two linked services and resource competition.
4. Challenge the favored scenario and treatment through an independent reviewer, alternative explanation, counterexample and sensitivity check. Record dissent.
5. Track weak signals and change triggers: law/code/adopted edition, land use, climate/hazard maps, ownership, staff/vendor, technology, infrastructure, funding, insurance and public-service capacity.
6. Set a named owner, review date or event trigger, collection action and next-level decision for each material unknown. The scope stops when additional collection no longer plausibly changes the Level 1 decision; Level 2/3 work requires approved scope and authority.

Apply proportionality: primary sources and authorized Member records first; specialist opinions when the issue requires licensed or qualified expertise; no unrestricted person investigation, intrusive testing, covert collection or unsupported local attribution.

## 7. Review control

At each new engagement and material change, the analyst logs search date, exact source set, jurisdiction, disciplines screened, excluded lenses with reason, unexamined archives/uploads, contradictions, reviewer competence, legal/specialist referral and next review trigger. A reviewer checks for missing affected groups and adverse effects. New cross-engagement lessons enter the SAL-D025 governed learning path with provenance, applicability, confidence, currency, restrictions, review and supersession. Human approval controls material method changes, final findings, publication, risk acceptance and identifiable disclosure. Protected Member records remain segregated.
